Here are the learning objectives of all of the chapters from HSC physics books : 
chapter 1.1 (Physical world and measurement) : 
1. Explain the nature of physical quantities.
2. Measure physical quantities and express the results appropriately.
3. Understand and explain the concepts of dimensions, formulas, principles, hypotheses, theories, and laws in physics.
4. Analyze the relationship between physics and other branches of knowledge.
5. Explain the cause-and-effect relationships among different topics, events, and processes in physics.
6. Explain the significance of measurement accuracy and precision.
7. Relate basic and derived physical quantities (units).
8. Explain the classification and importance of observations and measurements.
9. Use measuring instruments correctly.
10. Apply proper techniques to minimize errors in measurements.
Practical Skills:
* Measure the radius of curvature of a spherical surface using a spherometer.
* Determine the mass of an object using the oscillation method with a spring.


Chapter 1.2 (vector) :
1. Explain the properties of vectors.
2. Represent various physical quantities in physics as vectors.
3. Perform addition of vectors.
4. Explain the graphical methods of vector addition.
5. Analyze vector addition and subtraction using components.
6. Resolve a vector into rectangular (perpendicular) components.
7. Use the scalar and vector (dot and cross) products of two vectors.
8. Explain the importance and applications of vector resolution in physics.
9. Explain the multiplication of vectors.
10. Use vector operators in solving problems.




Chapter 1.3 (Dynamics) : 
1. Explain the concept of a frame of reference.
2. Explain the basic concepts of translational and rotational motion.
3. Analyze displacement–time and velocity–time graphs.
4. Analyze the motion of freely falling objects.
5. Analyze projectile motion.
6. Explain uniform circular motion.




Chapter 1.4 (Newtonian Mechanics) : 
1. Explain the fundamental concept of force.
2. Analyze Newton’s Second Law using calculus.
3. Explain the relationships among Newton’s laws of motion.
4. Apply Newton’s laws of motion to solve problems.
5. Explain the limitations of Newton’s laws of motion.
6. Explain the concepts of force, field, and interaction.
7. Explain the law of conservation of linear momentum.
8. Verify the conservation of momentum in all situations.
9. Analyze the relationship between Newton’s Third Law and the conservation of momentum.
10. Explain the concepts of torque and angular momentum.
11. Explain the law of conservation of angular momentum.
12. Explain torque.
13. Analyze the relationship among torque, angular momentum, and angular velocity.
14. Explain the conservation of angular momentum as a universal law.
15. Apply the concepts of centripetal and centrifugal forces.
16. Explain the necessity of banking on curved roads.
17. Explain static and kinetic friction.
18. Solve problems involving static friction between two objects.
Practical Skill:
* Determine the moment of inertia of a flywheel.




Chapter 1.5 (Work, Energy and Power) : 
1. Explain the general concepts of work and energy.
2. Analyze the relationship between force, displacement, and work.
3. Analyze the work done by constant and variable forces.
4. Compare the work done by conservative and non-conservative forces.
5. Derive the mathematical expression of the work–energy theorem and apply it to solve problems.
6. Derive the mathematical expression of the conservation of energy and apply it to solve problems.
7. Apply the principle of conservation of energy to solve various practical problems.
8. Analyze the relationship between momentum, work, and kinetic energy.
9. Explain conservative and non-conservative forces.
10. Calculate the efficiency of a system.
Practical Skill : 
* Measure the power output of a pump. 






Chapter 1.6 (Gravitation and Gravity) : 
1. Explain Galileo’s law for freely falling bodies.
2. State and explain Kepler’s laws of planetary motion.
3. Derive and analyze Kepler’s laws mathematically using Newton’s laws.
4. Establish the mathematical relationship between gravitational force and gravitational acceleration, and apply this relationship to solve problems.
5. Apply the law of universal gravitation.
6. Determine the magnitudes of gravitational force, gravitational field strength, and gravitational potential, and explain the relationships among them.
7. Analyze the causes of variations in gravitational acceleration.
8. Explain the concept of the center of gravity.
9. Derive and analyze the mathematical conditions of equilibrium.
10. Describe the applications of the law of universal gravitation.
Practical Skill:
*  Determine the acceleration due to gravity by measuring the distance and time of a freely falling object dropped through a solenoid coil.

1. Explain the nature of intermolecular forces in matter.
2. Explain the different types of bonding in matter.
3. Explain the elastic behavior of materials in terms of intermolecular forces.
4. Explain the quantities related to elasticity.
5. Explain Hooke’s Law.

8. Explain Poisson’s ratio.

11. Explain viscosity and the coefficient of viscosity.
12. Explain the relationship between friction and viscosity.
13. Explain Stokes’ law for a body moving through a fluid.
14. Apply Bernoulli’s principle.
15. Explain surface tension.
16. Explain cohesive and adhesive forces.
17. Explain the angle of contact.
18. Describe capillary action.
Practical Skill:

Chapter 1.8 (Periodic Motion): 
1. Explain the concept of periodic motion.

3. Explain the nature and characteristics of force in simple harmonic motion (SHM).
